# Research and page content

Determine what the audience should understand, do, judge or feel. Respect the actual
brief, page constraints and source scope. Do not turn policy research into a sales
pitch or a bounded performance review into a full biography.

Read sources, figures and examples; investigate important gaps when allowed. Prefer
primary evidence for facts and credible interpretations where appropriate. Record
useful evidence/conflicts, not copied search snippets. Stop when the essential
explanations, evidence and examples are supported; no search-count quota.

Depth varies with the task:
- Teaching: a difficult step, misconception and worked example with conditions;
  a usable explanation is valuable without a novel thesis.
- History/people/literature: actual work/events, context, choices and consequences;
  distinguish established fact, sources and interpretation.
- Products: real use case, change, evidence and limitations; units/baselines matter.
- Reports/plans: observations, causes, assumptions and decision implications; research
  reports need not follow corporate KPI structure.
- Other subjects: answer natural questions through mechanisms, cases and boundaries.
These are prompts, not chapter templates.

“Quadratic functions” is a topic label. “Compare y=x² and y=2x² on shared axes,
explain using the same x values, then ask the learner to predict another curve”
specifies teaching. Pages can provide evidence, examples, emotional turns or quiet
summaries; do not require all roles on each page or invent page-count filler.

outline.json: title, purpose, optional style, and ordered slides. Each slide has
stable id, title, summary and optional section. Array order is presentation order.
Keep IDs stable on reorder, never reuse them for unrelated content. Summary is
substantive reader-facing prose, not hidden reasoning, local paths or raw logs.
Longer detail stays in source-notes.md. Use the host publisher; synchronize revisions.

Preserve periods, units, denominators and qualifications. Check examples' arithmetic
and explanation, not just source-heading coverage. Do not compress away premises or
hide essential self-reading content in notes. Use the formula reference when needed.
Before building, read the sequence: can each page follow from prior knowledge,
does it add anything, and what expected question remains unanswered?
