# Independent content review

Use a fresh context without the author's conversation, outline, research notes,
rationale or self-assessment. Do not edit the deck.

First read every page in order from the actual audience surface. Self-reading must
work without hidden notes. A live deck may include a real supplied script; never
invent a presenter who fills every gap. Record concrete page evidence:
- Entry: purpose, starting point, unexplained terms/people/symbols/units.
- Continuity: missing premises, definitions, causal steps or unsupported conclusions.
- Substance: useful knowledge, evidence, method, judgment or earned emotion;
  paraphrased headings are not depth.
- Concreteness: examples, source passages, scenarios, comparisons or worked steps
  that actually explain the abstraction.
- Load: too many unfamiliar concepts together, or brevity removing explanation.
- Natural questions: conditions after benefits, application after methods, evidence
  after assertions, choices and consequences beyond chronology.
- Closing: specific understanding/feeling, answered promises, visible boundaries.

Only after first-pass findings inspect the original brief and necessary sources.
Check required coverage, accuracy, data/units, examples, quotes, uncertainty,
attribution and scope. Separate source errors from author errors. Do not demand
unrequested topics or novel opinions from a lesson.

Report pages, observation, audience consequence, evidence and repair. Mark required
corrections versus suggestions. State actual coverage and unreadable/untested parts.
No finding quota or numerical taste score. Text/thumbnails alone are insufficient.
Follow the host JSON schema where supplied. Recheck actual revised pages and their
context; an author's resolution claim is not verification.
