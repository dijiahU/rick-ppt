# Research and page content

Determine what the audience should understand, do, judge or feel. Respect the actual
brief, page constraints and source scope. Do not turn policy research into a sales
pitch or a bounded performance review into a full biography.

## Investigate questions before arranging pages

<!-- research-breadth/v1 -->

For open topic research/new design, do a broad first pass before choosing the
outline. Investigate factual evidence, real uses/outputs and on-slide assets, and
finished visual work; finding official documentation does not finish discovery.
Use useful Chinese and English queries, not just translated copies of a generic
topic. Batch independent hosted searches, then deepen promising different leads.
Keep the search breadth across visual treatments as well as websites. Include
finished examples for the intended use/audience alongside cross-medium inspiration;
do not append "editorial illustration" or another style term to every design query
before choosing a direction. For example, a professional adoption briefing can
learn from report spreads, product documentation and workplace imagery as well as
illustration. These are leads, not a whitelist; useful unexpected sources remain open.

For independent research questions, use the exposed `pptx_search_sources` batch
form `requests: ["...", "..."]` when available (up to three). Give each helper a
concrete evidence need rather than the entire presentation assignment. Facts and
mechanisms, real cases/material, and finished visual works are useful examples,
not compulsory roles. Helpers may investigate relevant public sites across
platforms; compare supported findings, original URLs and remaining gaps before
settling the outline. The browser allowlist does not limit ordinary public-web
discovery. Follow-up questions may depend on earlier evidence and remain sequential.

When capabilities.json exposes authorized browser sites, actually try each listed
channel with a task-specific query before narrowing: Pinterest for finished visual
work, X for public examples/processes, and Xiaohongshu for relevant Chinese examples
are possible entry questions, not exclusive roles or a source whitelist. Use the
exposed pptx_browser_sources with site explicitly set. When its schema advertises
requests, submit independent queries in one batch (up to three, each with its
own site/query/purpose); the host isolates sessions and tabs. When Google is
advertised, prefer its explicit search route for general discovery, including
different questions on Google concurrently. Do not use the browser's default
address-bar/Bing search. The hosted public search channel does not expose engine
selection; label it accurately rather than claiming it used Google. Platform-native
queries and permitted original-source reading complement discovery.
Legacy single-site calls remain available for dependent follow-up research.
The host's list is the
boundary; this instruction grants no browser/account/domain access. If that tool
is absent, use permitted public search and the current harness's real channels.
Keep offline/supplied-only requests and faithful/local edits within scope.

Keep each actual channel/query and useful/empty/blocked/unavailable outcome briefly
in the existing source notes. Batch independent web queries too. Let the host own
browser concurrency and cleanup rather than competing for a shared tab. Do not retry access denial,
login/captcha barriers or a confirmed shared-browser outage. A timeout or network
error on one site does not establish that another authorized site is unavailable;
follow the tool's site/browser failure scope and continue independent channels.
Such outcomes are limitations, not
successful inspection and not a reason to block the whole presentation. Compare
findings before selecting; no source from every channel, page-by-page search,
repeated exhaustive sweep or extra review gate is required. Follow original
sources for factual verification and inspect actual pixels for visual choices.

For topic_research, begin with provisional audience questions and broad discovery.
Look beyond the first definition or official overview for relevant uses, artifacts,
experiences, debates, visual material and connections to other fields. Follow a
promising unexpected lead when it could improve this presentation; findings can
change the questions, examples and proposed direction. Audience relevance guides
final selection without restricting discovery to an approved source/domain list.

Keep questions, useful discoveries and consequential gaps compactly in source-notes.md.
This research agenda is not a fixed slide outline or a search whitelist. Do not
search only to confirm early assumptions or fill generic chapters. Supplied-only
briefs, targeted verification and scoped edits retain their actual source/scope
limits; broad discovery is not permission to expand the user's assignment.

Read actual sources, figures and examples. Search for the question, not just the
topic name: its origin, mechanism, conditions, concrete instance, consequence or
competing explanation as relevant. Investigate relationships and change rather
than merely inventorying features, dates or places. These are useful directions,
not a checklist required for every subject or page.

Possible subject-specific starting points, not boundaries on what to search:
- Teaching: relevant disciplinary sources, prerequisites, concept relationships,
  difficult steps, misconceptions and worked examples with conditions. A usable
  explanation is valuable without a novel thesis.
- History/people/literature: original passages/works, their historical situation,
  the problem or dispute addressed, choices and consequences, and useful scholarly
  interpretations. Distinguish established fact, source perspective and interpretation.
- Products: official capability boundaries plus firsthand workflows, actual outputs,
  failures and costs when relevant. Explain the change from input to result;
  units/baselines matter. User reports establish reported experience, not universal
  performance or independently reproduced success.
- Places/routes/spatial proposals: actual site conditions, users, activities and
  movement; relevant landscape, architecture or public-space precedents when they
  help explain the proposal. Preserve real geography and distinguish observed
  conditions from proposed changes; do not substitute a landmark list for route logic.
- Reports/plans: observations, causes, assumptions, alternatives and decision
  implications; research reports need not follow corporate KPI structure.
- Other subjects: answer natural questions through mechanisms, cases and boundaries.
These are optional leads, not an exhaustive taxonomy, source whitelist or chapter
template. Combine fields or choose better leads as useful. For example, a product
may be understood through a creator's workflow or a compelling finished artifact;
a spatial proposal may benefit from observation, film, landscape or activity design.
The borrowed material still has to serve the audience and preserve source meaning.

Follow a useful case far enough to explain the important transition: the starting
problem/input, consequential actions or relationships, result and conditions or
limits. Inspect original outputs or process evidence where accessible. A detailed
case can explain a mechanism without proving that every case behaves the same way.
After discovering promising material, deepen the questions and cases that matter.
Do not let the first available official page or academic example determine the
whole deck merely because it is easy to cite. Its explanatory value and audience
fit matter alongside credibility. Prefer resolving a useful unknown to collecting
many repetitive overview pages.
Choose different perspectives or counterexamples when they could change the
conclusion; multiple reposts or agents summarizing one source are not independent
confirmation. Parallel helpers can investigate independent questions; the main
author compares evidence and owns the synthesis and page sequence.

## Synthesize, then plan the explanation

<!-- design-task/v1 -->

After research, turn the user's brief into a compact, actionable design assignment
in the existing source notes. Keep the original request intact. Separate explicit
requirements, supported facts and proposed design choices; proposals are revisable,
not extra user constraints. Explain what this audience should understand, recognize
or decide, and why the proposed viewing experience serves that outcome. A short
request needs professional interpretation, not invented facts or a longer sales pitch.

For each consequential page task, reuse the `pptx-handoff` fields in page-design.md:
`question` states the audience's task; `evidence` / `materials` identify real support
and file/viewing status; `preserve` keeps actual conditions; `uncertainties` names
missing support. In `visual_opportunity`, connect a plausible expression to an
observable completion condition: what must be visible for this page to do its job?
For example, if a decision depends on fitting a device into a small space, a weight
and dimensions list may not establish the footprint; a labeled concept view or a
dimensioned native view could. This is a task-dependent choice, not a rule that all
product pages need a rendering. A definition or exact quotation may need only a
short hierarchy task. Do not turn every field into a paragraph or add a new form.

For an audience unfamiliar with the subject, identify what they would otherwise
have to imagine from the prose: an object, operation, state change, spatial relation
or comparison. Prepare a usable on-slide picture or the evidence needed to draw
that explanation, in the same materials/visual_opportunity fields.
For new decks, prepare pictorial expression for the main content that benefits
from being seen, across the ordered pages/sections rather than just one token
picture. Decide the visible subject first, then choose authentic imagery, exposed
generation, native illustration or a hybrid. Do not settle every page as prose
and add an image at the end. Native explanatory drawings qualify;
identify their actual evidence and intended role in the existing handoff. Follow
art-direction.md's visual-presence requirement and its faithful/scoped exceptions.
A useful image
does not have to be indispensable to earn space. Search for explanatory figures,
actual outputs and inspectable examples as well as style inspiration. If the
original cannot be used, keep its explanatory job open for a permitted alternative,
native schematic or labeled original illustration. Data charts remain useful for
quantitative evidence but do not satisfy the pictorial requirement. "No required photo" and
"text is available" do not resolve that job. Keep the benefit, action or mechanism
requested by the user central; qualifications accompany it without turning every
page into a repeated caution unless that is the actual brief.

Use the existing deck-wide design_direction for a provisional visual direction
and its audience rationale. Carry explicit requested and rejected treatments from
the brief/style and current revisions into the existing preserve/handoff notes,
with their scope intact. Do not summarize them away as "professional" or "no default
style"; they constrain the designer's choice. Do not invent a restriction where
the user supplied none. Separate the visible task from the proposed treatment:
"show which fields are checked" does not prescribe a magnifying-glass cartoon.
Connect imagery, color and shape/texture to the requested experience, not just the
subject; distinguish explicit tone/brand requirements from provisional inference.
An unspecified style does not make the stated use irrelevant. Ease of understanding
and a playful appearance are different choices. Subject
labels alone do not select a palette or style.
Include actual finished-deck/template references when useful to choosing the shared
background and foreground relationship. Search those alongside usable subject
imagery, not in place of it. Hand off inspected examples and transferable decisions;
include the concrete visible treatment that makes an example useful for this task,
not only "hierarchy" or "grouping". Select by the unresolved visual work: a respected
studio's small icon overview is not automatically a useful model for a strong
subject image or image/type composition. A reference's actual color, background or
image treatment is available to the designer when it fits; do not add an inferred
ban on those features merely to avoid a preset style. Leave final selection,
adaptation and page exceptions to the continuing designer. Do not turn the short
brief into a fixed aesthetic recipe or add a reference quota.
Do not prescribe coordinates, image counts or identical page treatments. State
what remains to be resolved with actual material; do not certify a rough substitute
as finished. The continuing author may improve the proposed expression, and review
must judge the resulting audience experience rather than compliance with the plan.

In the same source notes, connect the important findings to evidence and their
consequences for this presentation: what was found, how/why it works where known,
which example makes it concrete, and what remains uncertain. Keep concise source
locations and the distinction between evidence, interpretation and illustration;
these are usable findings, not a private reasoning transcript or a second report.
Prefer primary evidence for facts and credible interpretation where appropriate.
Search snippets, reading counts and completed downloads do not establish understanding.

For new decks/substantive redesigns with creative latitude, use the existing
investigation to discover relevant finished visual work alongside factual evidence.
Combine the topic and intended visual problem with useful creator/work vocabulary:
editorial or scientific illustration, exhibition graphics, landscape cutaways,
information design or another appropriate field. These are search leads, not a
source whitelist. An installation diagram may settle geometry while offering
little help with subject construction, material distinction or image/type craft.
Inspect the actual picture for the problem it can resolve: subject construction,
spatial layering, focal scale, annotation or image/type relationships, for example.
A photograph of a completed project can inform scene, material or crop; it does
not by itself demonstrate a finished explanatory graphic. A technical figure can
settle a mechanism without calibrating the desired illustration craft. Describe
the useful visible operation, beyond listing the objects shown. A crop, technical
fragment or rough study can inform a limited relationship without demonstrating
the finished whole; keep that limit in existing observations. Inspiration need not become an embedded
asset; keep acquisition/embedding permissions distinct from the borrowed principle.
Use existing observed/borrowed/applications
records and usable files, leaving final composition to the designer. No required
artist, reference count, per-page search or extra approval stage; preserve actual
source/template/edit limits and retain missing/unviewed states honestly.

Build the ordered outline from that synthesis and the audience's starting knowledge.
Choose what each page helps the audience understand and what they need to see;
plan content and expression together without prewriting equally sized paragraphs.
Hand over the supported conclusion/relationship, exact evidence locations, useful
actual material with its intended role, necessary conditions and consequential
unknowns where relevant. For a selected case or mechanism, retain a compact,
attributed original fragment/detail that the audience could inspect: a rule,
observed input/change, data segment, original phrase, useful figure region or
concrete output. Put its exact source location and actual viewing/file status in
these same notes; a summary can accompany it. Do not replace all useful specificity
with component names or benefit claims. Preserve source values/conditions and
mark proposed teaching illustrations separately; do not invent unavailable output
screens, measurements or historical images. This is selective evidence preparation,
not a requirement to collect a picture or quote for every page. Use existing visual_opportunity/plain notes to say what
the audience should recognize, compare or trace, leaving wording and composition
to the designer. Distinguish actual user/source constraints from proposed visual
treatments. Do not declare a simple representation sufficient or exclude a
truthful alternative merely because the content can be summarized that way;
the designer tests the expression against actual material and renders.
Identify what actual material supports that visual task and which
gap still matters. A missing photograph may leave recognition unresolved even when
a factual path diagram is possible; do not silently declare the simpler substitute
equivalent. Seek a useful permitted original/alternative or reconsider an unfixed
case/expression before committing it. Preserve specified cases and factual scope;
no per-page material requirement or new handoff form. For material-rich pages,
[page evidence](page-design.md) offers optional indexed pointers inside these same
notes. Plain notes and a supported summary remain sufficient for simple pages.
Write each detailed evidence passage once in these notes with an exact source
locator; other page handoffs can point to it instead of repeating the passage.
Keep the public outline's summaries substantive and references' observed/borrowed/
applications in the existing decision register. Do not duplicate that register or
expand every optional handoff field into another narrative. Preserve unique facts,
qualifications, useful examples and build semantics. When notes already exist,
planning_commit accepts source_notes_file so the final submission need not rewrite
them into JSON. This changes recording overhead, not the breadth of discovery.
If a text-only case is worth explaining, an original conceptual illustration,
labeled illustrative sequence or native editable example can make it visible.
It must preserve the supported mechanism and not masquerade as the real output or
prove a reported result. Follow research-and-assets.md for documentary constraints.

Stop initial research when essential explanations and useful selected material
support a truthful, audience-relevant outline and further exploration adds little.
An unresolved consequential visual need remains part of the handoff even if the
outline is factually supported; source-search completion is not usable-asset or
design completion. Repetition within one narrow source family is not evidence that the subject is
exhausted: change vocabulary, angle or source community when a valuable question
or visual need remains unresolved. No requirement to search every channel or chase
unrelated novelty. Keep nonessential later-page
acquisition asynchronous where available. During sequential authoring, target new
research at a concrete missing fact, explanation or material need and update the
relevant notes/outline; do not restart the whole investigation. No fixed reading
time, source count, image quota or additional approval/review stage.

“Quadratic functions” is a topic label. “Compare y=x² and y=2x² on shared axes,
explain using the same x values, then ask the learner to predict another curve”
specifies teaching. Pages can provide evidence, examples, emotional turns or quiet
summaries; do not require all roles on each page or invent page-count filler.

outline.json: title, purpose, optional style, and ordered slides. Each slide has
stable id, title, summary and optional section. Array order is presentation order.
Keep IDs stable on reorder, never reuse them for unrelated content. Summary is
substantive reader-facing prose, not hidden reasoning, local paths or raw logs.
Longer detail stays in source-notes.md. Use the host publisher; synchronize revisions.

Preserve periods, units, denominators and qualifications. Check examples' arithmetic
and explanation, not just source-heading coverage. Do not compress away premises or
hide essential self-reading content in notes. Use the formula reference when needed.
Before building, read the sequence: can each page follow from prior knowledge,
does it add anything, and what expected question remains unanswered?
