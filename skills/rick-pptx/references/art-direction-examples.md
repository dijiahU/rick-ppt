# Examples of design decisions, not templates

Read a relevant example when turning content into a visual expression is difficult.
They show operations and their limits; they prescribe no chapter structure, artist,
layout or picture count for unrelated tasks.

## The same material, different requested experiences

Brief A asks people to inspect how a water-treatment system works and question its
measured performance. A restrained frame gives space to the section, attached
operating conditions and comparable results; a giant capacity headline would distract
from the mechanism, so reduce it while keeping quantity and unit. A row of component
names would lose the explanation — keep essential paths and boundaries precise.

Brief B asks visitors to grasp the visible transformation from a distance before a
live explanation: an inspected before/after image or bold cross-section can lead, with
details arriving as the talk develops. Neither follows from the subject being
scientific, public or commercial; use the requested experience, evidence and viewing.

Rendered check: does the strongest treatment serve that task? If image, heading,
numbers and conclusion compete, quiet the supporting elements; if the frame is calm but
the mechanism abstract, develop the explanation. Small type, empty space, muted color
and spectacle are not universal quality signals; a user reference guides
relationships, not a page template.

## A shared background, changing subjects

A reference deck may hold pages together through one background relationship and a
recurring type/image treatment. Inspect a substantive page as well as the cover: does
the identity survive real content, and what changes when a photograph, dense comparison
or concluding statement leads? Name the responsible treatment — a photograph's occupied
area, crop shape, a dark/light boundary, type placement and density. Its palette or
background may also fit this brief. Adapt the selected decisions to the current
subject; three text boxes in a shared frame do not reproduce an image-led reference,
and no palette or arrangement becomes a default for unrelated tasks.

Rendered check: put the real subject into the treatment. If the background competes,
flattens the focal structure or pushes material into small boxes, adjust that
relationship while keeping imagery, scale and click groups. If another treatment suits
a particular subject better, keep the shared cues for coherence rather than force every
page into one frame.

## Scientific mechanism: a check before a measurement

A source describes a sample checked against calibration data before it is measured or
returned for preparation (hypothetical; supplies no result — use the paper's actual
actors, order and conditions). The audience must see what is checked, what informs the
decision and what happens next.

A first draft with equal boxes for "sample", "calibration", "check" and "measurement"
joined at nearest edges has correct labels and endpoints but does not separate the
sample's journey from the evidence used by the check. One construction follows the
sample through check to outcome, with calibration evidence attached to the decision it
informs; plan path, branch space and nodes before coordinates, put each condition at
its branch and keep outcome labels on deliberate baselines. A very short process may
need only two aligned states with an annotation; a spatial apparatus a native cutaway.

Rendered check: can you follow the sample without the paragraph and tell its movement
from supporting evidence? Do lines have room, does each label clearly belong to one
object/branch, does the focal step have scale? An arbitrary but correct fan of lines
may need recomposition — recoloring arrows does not fix it. Ordinary branches use
deliberate horizontal/vertical routes with clear weight; keep oblique paths that encode
real spatial/directional meaning or required source geometry. Reveal related objects,
labels and connections together without losing context.

## Research evidence: a full table with a clear argument

Question: which observations support the result, and where are its limits? Means,
uncertainty, baselines, configurations and units may all be needed; a large claim over
equally weighted cells leaves the viewer to find the proof. One design keeps the
complete comparable table with a quiet consistent baseline treatment, draws attention
to the pairs the conclusion discusses and puts the increment/unit beside them; click
groups guide the live sequence while the final view retains all evidence. Other data
may suit aligned plots or small multiples — choose from the comparison, with honest
scales and uncertainty; do not force tables into charts.

Rendered check: can the audience find the evidence, compare like with like and see the
qualification? Is emphasis earned and coherent with related result pages (not a new
highlight system per page)?

## A historical text: connect exact words with authentic evidence

Question: what argument is made, in what situation, and what supports the
interpretation? Exact passage and context matter more than atmosphere; use verified
wording and authentic documents/photos when they exist. A page may give a short sourced
passage strong typographic presence beside an authentic document view, enlarge a
verified detail and connect text, situation and interpretation with short native
annotations; another may be a designed quotation alone when no useful image exists —
reading path and hierarchy can make a text-led page complete. A photographic/editorial
reference may inform crop, tonal areas or large-quote/small-context relations using
this subject's evidence. Never generate an archival photograph, invent a marked passage
or imply a quote appears in the shown document without checking.

Rendered check: are original words, analysis and the image's purpose distinguishable?
Is the passage readable and connected? A dramatic crop must not erase the evidence's
identity.

## Product or proposed experience: make the output visible

Question: what can a person actually do or experience, and what changes? A benefits list
leaves the result invisible. For a real product show an inspected actual screen or
output at useful scale with editable annotations on the relevant behavior. For a
hypothetical proposal an original concept illustration may show the setting or effect,
labeled illustrative, with analytical relationships and quantities native; use
sources/generation per the brief and tools. A photographic/film reference could inform
foreground/background, selective focus or a quiet label area. The picture must take part
in the explanation — a tiny image beside unchanged paragraphs rarely changes the reading
task; some pages need a native permissions or cost diagram instead.

Rendered check: is the subject/output visible, and real evidence distinguishable from a
proposed scene? Does the image give its labels enough detail, and does the page still
belong to the technical explanation around it?

## Continuity: overview, detail and evidence

A sequence may introduce a system, enlarge one component to expose its mechanism, then
return to stable quantitative comparison, reusing the component's identity, color
meaning and annotation grammar while scale and density change; a shared path, baseline
or object carries the transition. No identical container grid and no new metaphor per
topic are needed; other narratives may need another sequence.

E.g. a mechanism separates detecting a discrepancy from correcting it; a following case
listing file names and a success score hides that distinction. Select a
source-supported discrepancy or rule application, expose its consequence, and keep the
two operations identifiable through labels, alignment or another relationship, without
recopying the overview. If the source gives only a rule, show that exact evidence; use a
labeled teaching instance only where allowed, never a fabricated result. Rendered check:
can the audience point to each necessary operation and its supporting detail? Source
notes may document provenance but must not be the only home of this mapping.

When studying a reference series, examine what repeats and what changes; borrow a
continuity principle, test it on current pages and record its concrete application.
Palette values or artist names alone do not supply continuity.

## When the first idea should be abandoned

A researcher suggests a chronological strip for a report with several case studies, but
the material shows independent cases whose evidence is large photographs with different
details. Tiny photos with long captions imply an unsupported sequence and hide the
details; connectors or smaller captions will not fix that. Compose the first case
around an inspectable photograph with native evidence annotations, relate the other
cases through a stable label/color grammar, update the direction and affected
asset/page notes, and keep dates, identities, evidence limits and protected template
elements. Another subject may truly need the strip — the decision follows the
relationship and material, not a preference for photographs.

Authoring in order, the first case render exposes this early; later previews may reveal
other mismatches. Change only the affected treatment and reuse what works — neither
repeat a failed scheme nor restart completed pages whenever an alternative is imaginable.
